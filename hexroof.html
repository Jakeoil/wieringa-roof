Hexahedra

The roof as a solid slab rather than a surface. Under every rhomb hangs exactly one golden hexahedron — acute beneath the thick, obtuse beneath the thin, straight down, with nothing to choose. The cells carry their own upper and lower surfaces, so the roof itself is an accessory here: leave it off, or drop it over the model as a transparent color filter.

How it works

The change of primitive. At the surface the roof is golden rhombs — a Penrose P3 tiling lifted out of the plane. One level down the natural blocks are not rhombs at all but the two golden hexahedra, acute and obtuse, the three-dimensional analogues of the thick and thin tiles and the cells of icosahedral quasicrystal models.

One hexahedron per rhomb, and nothing to choose. A rhomb spans two of the five lifting axes. The third edge of its cell is the vertical — the sixth icosahedral axis, the one the roof surface itself never uses. Every generator satisfies |Ej| = 1 and Ej · ez = 1/√5 exactly, so {Ej, Ek, ez} is a golden rhombohedron like any other triple of the six. The cell hangs straight down: four plumb side walls, the rhomb on top and its own translate beneath.

Thick gives acute, thin gives obtuse. For a thick rhomb the two lifting axes meet at +1/√5, so all three pairwise dots are positive and the cell is prolate, volume 0.760845. For a thin rhomb that dot is negative and the cell is oblate, 0.470228. The acute-to-obtuse ratio is therefore the thick-to-thin ratio, tending to φ — and the two volumes are themselves in golden ratio.

They cannot overlap. Not by search but by construction: each cell is a vertical prism under its own rhomb, and the rhombs project to a tiling of the plane. Disjoint shadows, therefore disjoint prisms, whatever their heights.

The two heights

Everything on this page is measured in units of the rhomb edge, and that edge is the object of length 1. All six icosahedral generators are unit vectors, the vertical included, so every edge of every hexahedron — all twelve — is 1, and so is the drop from the roof to the floor. The slab is exactly one rhomb edge thick.

The lift's own step is the other height. A vertex of index m sits at z = m/√5, because each generator contributes Ej · ez = 1/√5, and the four indices of a Penrose lift give four levels:

That is irrational, and it has a consequence: the floor never lands on a height the roof uses. Not rarely — never, since m − m′ = √5 has no integer solution.

Isoglosses, and where they mean something

A roof edge changes the index by exactly 1, so every rhomb spans exactly 2 levels and contours drawn on eighths of a face's own span are quarter-index levels globally — they run on continuously from one rhomb into the next. On the cell tops that is the roof's own contour map, and on the floors it is the same map exactly one unit lower.

The side walls get none, and for a reason rather than for taste. A wall is plumb, so height restricted to it depends on nothing but z: its level sets are horizontal lines, on every wall alike. They would be dense, uniform, and would tell you only that the wall is vertical, which the construction already guarantees. What the walls really encode is the √5 above — band them by height and you would see bands registering with nothing, ever, in either direction. Better said in a sentence than drawn.

The controls

The hexahedra are the subject and come first. Color and surface are separate: how a cell is painted has nothing to do with whether it is solid, transparent or hidden. Tops and floors turn the cells' own two Penrose surfaces on and off — they belong to the cells, not to the roof. Shading ramps the tops and floors light-high to dark-low as seen, so it inverts under tails, and that inversion is the cue that the model has been turned over.

Every generation is offered, and the ones that will not hold up are ghosted. A single cap would be the wrong instrument: Pe5 reaches generation 6 at 33,820 rhombs while Sun is already at 16,475 by generation 4, so one number that suits the largest patch throws away most of what the small ones can do. Each entry carries its rhomb count, is marked (slow) past 8,000, and is disabled past 45,000 — the page pays for each cell in six faces and twenty-four edge segments, and the edges are the expensive half. The three star seeds show generation 1 as none, since a star, boat or diamond is not produced until the first deflation.

Parity is tails and heads, and there is no flat roof here. The reflection is exact — a mirrored golden rhombohedron is still golden — but the vertical-scale slider of the 3D page is not available here, because squashing the lift would leave the solids no longer golden, and the solids are the point. The roof overlay may be collapsed instead: collapse flattens it into its own shadow, the plain Penrose tiling, and holds it above the model as a sheet — flatten, which collapses the surface, not the panel.

The roof is an accessory. Off by default, since the cells already carry their own upper and lower surfaces — and its whole control group folds away, with the current setting shown on the closed header so nothing is hidden by being put out of sight. Its use is as a transparent film for reading the tiling's coloring through the solid. Its rhombs are coplanar with the cell tops point for point, so it is lifted a hair clear of them along the outward direction — two exactly coincident surfaces cannot be depth-sorted, and left alone they mottle together and neither reads.
