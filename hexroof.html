Hexahedra

The same roof, seen as solid rather than as surface. Every rhomb is a face of a golden rhombohedron, and this hangs one beneath each rhomb it can. Start with the roof in the cluster colours or the Kowalewski five, then bring the hexahedra up.

How it works

The change of primitive. At the surface the roof is golden rhombs — a Penrose P3 tiling lifted out of the plane. One level down the natural blocks are not rhombs at all but the two golden rhombohedra, acute and obtuse, which are the three-dimensional analogues of the thick and thin tiles and the cells of icosahedral quasicrystal models. The same object, two equally natural descriptions — much as P1 and P3 are.

The local fit is exact, with no fudging. The lift uses five generators whose pairwise angles can only be 63.4349° or 116.5651° — precisely the angles of the golden rhombus. So any three of them span a parallelepiped with six congruent golden-rhomb faces, which is to say one of the two golden rhombohedra, prolate or oblate according to the signs. Every roof rhomb is therefore a face of one, exactly.

The global fit is the open question, and it does not close. A rhomb spans two axes; hanging a cell beneath it means choosing a third from the remaining three, and the cells must not overlap. Greedily, most-constrained first, about 57% of rhombi can be given one — and that fraction holds from generation 2 to generation 4, so it is structural and not an artifact of the patch boundary. Colour the roof by carries a hexahedron to see which do and which do not.

A greedy assignment is not a proof of impossibility, and this is stated as measured rather than settled. But it does answer the guess in the source discussion, which offered three possibilities — that the opposite faces mesh exactly, that they almost mesh, or that they do not — and expected the middle one. The middle one is what the numbers say.

What that leaves. If the layer were complete, the far faces of the cells would form a second Penrose surface beneath the first, and the roof would be a quasiperiodic sandwich panel. It is not complete under this construction, so either a cleverer choice of third axes exists, or the roof is a slab boundary in a sense that does not reduce to one cell per rhomb. Both remain open.
