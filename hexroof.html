Hexahedra

The same roof, seen as solid rather than as surface. Under every rhomb hangs exactly one golden hexahedron — acute beneath the thick, obtuse beneath the thin, straight down, with nothing to choose. Start with the roof in the cluster colors or the Kowalewski five, then bring the hexahedra up.

How it works

The change of primitive. At the surface the roof is golden rhombs — a Penrose P3 tiling lifted out of the plane. One level down the natural blocks are not rhombs at all but the two golden hexahedra, acute and obtuse, which are the three-dimensional analogues of the thick and thin tiles and the cells of icosahedral quasicrystal models.

One hexahedron per rhomb, and nothing to choose. A rhomb spans two of the five lifting axes. The third edge of its cell is the vertical — which is the sixth icosahedral axis, the one the roof surface itself never uses. Every generator satisfies |Ej| = 1 and Ej · ez = 1/√5 exactly, so {Ej, Ek, ez} is a golden rhombohedron like any other triple of the six. The cell hangs straight down: four vertical side faces, edges running plumb, the rhomb on top and its own translate beneath.

Thick gives acute, thin gives obtuse. For a thick rhomb the two lifting axes meet at +1/√5, so all three pairwise dots are positive and the cell is the prolate one, volume 0.760845. For a thin rhomb that dot is negative and the cell is oblate, 0.470228. So the acute-to-obtuse ratio is just the thick-to-thin ratio, tending to φ.

They cannot overlap. Not by search but by construction: each cell is a vertical prism under its own rhomb, and the rhombi project to a tiling of the plane. Disjoint shadows, therefore disjoint prisms, whatever their heights.

And the floor is the roof again. The cells' bottom faces are their top faces moved down by one unit, so the second Penrose surface underneath is congruent to the first and exactly parallel — the quasiperiodic sandwich panel of the original discussion, and it is exact rather than approximate. Turn on floor to see it.
