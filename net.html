Net & PDF

Choose a patch, unfold it flat, and print a sheet to cut and fold. Each patch is an independent model, printable on its own whenever you want to build another.

Page not built yet — but the unfolding works and is committed. Run node tools/bfs-unfold.mjs for the numbers below. What remains is rendering the placed rhombi as printable SVG.

Gen-2 results

Breadth-first unfolding from a seed rhomb, spreading outward across shared edges and rejecting any placement that would overlap. At a 20 mm side, on Letter with half-inch margins:

Star Pe5252122 × 139 mmyes

Diamond Pe1211113 × 121 mmyes

Boat and diamond unfold whole. The star always splits, and trying all 25 rhombi as the starting seed never avoids it — but the leftover is a single rhombus, so in practice it is a scrap to cut separately rather than a second net.

Generation 3 also works, at 138–140 rhombi and four pieces, with the largest holding about 117 of them. It needs a smaller side — around 12 mm — to fit a sheet.

Scale

Nothing is tied to a particular size. Side length is a free parameter, in millimetres or inches, and page size and margin are options too: 

node tools/bfs-unfold.mjs
node tools/bfs-unfold.mjs --side=1in --page=a4
node tools/bfs-unfold.mjs --gen=3 --side=12mm

A 20 mm side folds crisply in ordinary 100–120 gsm office paper and is a reasonable default, but the geometry is scale-free — the only real limit is that the 108° creases get fiddly below about 12 mm.

Annotation

Every interior edge of the surface is a genuine crease — there are no flat joins — so each one gets its fold angle marked, always 36°, 72° or 108°, plus mountain or valley. Cut lines solid, creases dashed by angle. Output will be SVG at exact physical units with a print stylesheet, then the browser's own Save as PDF: no PDF library, and reliable 1:1 scale.
