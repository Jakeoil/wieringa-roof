Net & PDF

Every piece below is cut from the same golden rhombus. Solid lines are cuts, dashed lines are creases — dash length gives the fold angle, colour gives mountain or valley. Sheets are drawn at true size, so Print → Save as PDF gives you 1:1.

Folding

Score every dashed line and fold it to its marked angle — 36°, 72° or 108°, and nothing else. There are no flat joins on this surface, so every interior line really is a crease. Red is a ridge (mountain), blue a trough (valley); turning a whole piece over swaps them, so if a piece is easier to fold the other way up, that is fine.

Where a patch comes apart into more than one piece it is because the surface has saddle vertices — more than 360° of paper wants to meet at a point, so the faces around it cannot all stay joined. Join those pieces along their matching cut edges when assembling. See Mathematics for why.
