Unfolding Workbench

Two ways to work. Build by hand: click any rhomb to seed a net, then click rhombs touching it and each is unfolded across the shared edge — you choose the route. Watch an algorithm: replay one of the automatic methods step by step and see the same choices being made for you.

Click a rhomb to place it; click an edge to choose the hinge
Green means a route lands clean; red means every route from there overlaps. The preview runs each hinge a rhomb could arrive on and tests it before you commit, so the dead ends are visible in advance. Watch the red spread as you work round a saddle vertex — that is the surface's curvature showing up as a constraint on what you are allowed to do next.

Hover shows the ghosts. A rhomb touching the net on more than one edge has more than one possible landing place, and they can disagree — one hinge clean, another overlapping. Hovering draws all of them; hovering the edge you want narrows it to that one, and clicking the edge commits it. Clicking the rhomb instead takes its most recent neighbour.

Hovering either canvas highlights the twin in the other, with a marker at the corresponding point inside it. The two rhombi are not the same shape — 72° against 63.43° — so the pointer is carried across in the rhombus's own edge coordinates, which tracks corners and edges exactly.

The net is oriented and centred on the sheet automatically. The best orientation is found by testing the net's own edge directions and their perpendiculars — the minimum enclosing rectangle always has a side flush with a hull edge, and a development only ever uses about nine directions, so this is an exhaustive check of a couple of dozen angles rather than a search. A net too big for the printable frame is still drawn, spilling past it, with the frame turning pink and telling you how far over: snip along it. The Side box sets the rhombus edge, which is the real lever — at the default 1.118" a sheet holds about twenty rhombi.

Watching a replay. Play, or step with ← and →, or drag the scrubber; space toggles play. The tiling shows the search's own state — yellow placed, red tried and rejected for overlap, violet the move being made right now, with its candidate ghosted on the sheet. The replay is not a re-enactment: the algorithms emit a step log as they run, and this reads it back, so what you see is the very run that produced the net. The view is fitted once to the finished net and then held, since re-fitting each frame makes the picture lurch.

Creases are dashed by fold angle — 36°, 72°, 108° — and coloured red for mountain, blue for valley. Solid black edges are cuts. Overlapping placements are still allowed: they go down faded with a red dashed outline. Alt-click removes — in either canvas, so the modifier means the same thing wherever you are; held down, the placed rhombi turn red to show what it would take. Plain clicking the work canvas removes too, and Undo or ⌘Z steps back. Since hinges are what hold the net together, taking out an interior rhomb can leave two islands that only look like one net — you will be told when that happens. Hills up / Dales up flips the roof vertically, reversing every height and every crease. All seven seeds are here, including Deca.
