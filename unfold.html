Workbench

Watch branch-cut routing lay a net out step by step — or take over at any point and choose the route yourself.

How it works
Green means a route lands clean; red means every route from there overlaps. The preview runs each hinge a rhomb could arrive on and tests it before you commit, so the dead ends are visible in advance. Watch the red spread as you work round a saddle vertex — that is the surface's curvature showing up as a constraint on what you are allowed to do next.

Hover shows the ghosts. A rhomb touching the net on more than one edge has more than one possible landing place, and they can disagree — one hinge clean, another overlapping. Hovering draws all of them; hovering the edge you want narrows it to that one, and clicking the edge commits it. Clicking the rhomb instead takes its most recent neighbour.

Hovering either canvas highlights the twin in the other, with a marker at the corresponding point inside it. The two rhombi are not the same shape — 72° against 63.43° — so the pointer is carried across in the rhombus's own edge coordinates, which tracks corners and edges exactly.

The net is oriented and centred on the sheet automatically. The best orientation is found by testing the net's own edge directions and their perpendiculars — the minimum enclosing rectangle always has a side flush with a hull edge, and a development only ever uses about nine directions, so this is an exhaustive check of a couple of dozen angles rather than a search. A net too big for the printable frame is still drawn, spilling past it, with the frame turning pink and telling you how far over: snip along it. The Side box sets the rhombus edge, which is the real lever — at the default 1 in, which is the rhombus edge, a sheet holds about twenty rhombi. Every generation-2 patch fits one Letter sheet at that size.

Watching a replay. Play, or step with ← and →, or drag the scrubber; space toggles play. While it runs, the tiling shows the search's own state — yellow placed, red tried and rejected for overlap, violet the move being made right now, with its candidate ghosted on the sheet. Those are colours about the run, so when it finishes they go and the tiling returns to whichever colour you chose. The replay is not a re-enactment: the search emits a step log as it runs and this reads it back, so what you see is the very run that produced the net. The view is fitted once to the finished net and then held, since re-fitting each frame makes the picture lurch.

Play is a traffic light. White waiting, yellow running, green finished. Pressing it while it is yellow queues the split, so a long replay can be left to run and will arrive at sheets on its own; pressing again cancels that. Pause has its own button, because at generation 3 a replay is some nine hundred steps and stopping to look at one is the whole reason to watch.

You can take over at any point. The canvas is always live — click to place, alt-click to remove — and there is no mode to switch first. The one consequence is that the scrubber describes the log, not the canvas, so the first edit of your own detaches it: the transport goes away and Play changes to offer running the search again, which would replace what you built and therefore asks first.

Create sheets splits the finished net across pages. A generation-3 net is several times the size of a sheet, so at that scale this is what makes it buildable rather than merely correct. The split always runs along a hinge, never through a rhombus, so every rhombus lands whole on one sheet. Each severed hinge becomes a taped join carrying a capital letter and the number of the sheet its other half is on — a rhomb reading A▸3 tapes to the rhomb reading A▸1 on sheet 3. Every page shares one orientation, so you can lay the printed sheets on a table and see them line up. Because the hinges form a spanning tree, pages are always joins + 1: there is no trade-off between fewer sheets and fewer joins, and both are minimised together. Each join is drawn as a tab outside the cut, shaped like the rhombus it joins to — because a rhombus's adjacent angles are supplementary, truncating that neighbour at text height gives a parallelogram, so the tab is literally a slice of the piece that continues elsewhere. The letter runs parallel to the cut. Every sheet also carries a thumbnail of the whole net with that sheet shaded and every join marked, placed in whichever corner the net leaves clearest, so you can see at a glance which end you are holding.

Print sheets / PDF outputs the lot; Save as PDF gives one file. Changing the net discards the sheets, since they described a net that no longer exists.

Create sheets is the second traffic light. Red nothing to split yet, yellow splitting, green done — and once it is green it stops being a verb and becomes the way through to the Sheets view. It does not throw you there by itself: going green is the answer, and whether you want to look yet is your business. Sheets is the same page, so nothing is re-computed and switching back is instant. Once a net is split, the tiling canvas here shows the partition, each region in its sheet's colour: the same picture you built on, now divided.

Print net is the overview instead: the whole net on one page, shrunk however far it takes to fit.

Layer appears only when a net needs more than one sheet. Where the surface wraps over itself it need not be cut — the offending part continues on the next layer, which is the next sheet of paper, and every layer is flat by construction. Choosing one draws it solid and ghosts the rest, so you can see where the sheet you are about to print sits inside the whole net. All layers share one coordinate frame, so the printed sheets register against each other and the surface visibly continues across the seam. Up to generation 3 every patch is a single layer and the control stays hidden.

Type, Gen and Side sit above the tabs rather than inside a view: the patch is the subject of all of them, so it stays put when you switch, and you can step through seeds and generations while looking at whichever view answers the question. Side belongs there too — it decides how many sheets the net needs, so the Sheets view shows the same field rather than a second one that could disagree. Changing the patch on P1/P3 does not run a search, since there is no net on screen there to route.

Colour switches the tiling between the gen-1 clusters, the two mosaic palettes, thick against thin, and height index. Height is one control doing one job: its sign flips the lift, hills for dales, and its magnitude is how hard height is shaded, with the middle leaving the tiles flat. Both are display only and will not throw away the net you are working on. Print / PDF outputs what you have built, as vector at true size, through the sheet renderer in sheet.ts.

Creases are dashed by fold angle — 36°, 72°, 108° — and coloured red for mountain, blue for valley. Solid black edges are cuts. Overlapping placements are still allowed: they go down faded with a red dashed outline. Alt-click removes — in either canvas, so the modifier means the same thing wherever you are; held down, the placed rhombi turn red to show what it would take. Plain clicking the work canvas removes too, and Undo or ⌘Z steps back. Since hinges are what hold the net together, taking out an interior rhomb can leave two islands that only look like one net — you will be told when that happens. All nine seeds are here — the three Pe pentagons, the St star, boat and diamond, and the three composites Queen, Sun and Star — and the patch controls stay live while watching, so you can switch seed or generation mid-replay. Doing so starts the search again and discards any sheets, since they described a net that no longer exists.

Penrose Tiling

Net — not to scale. Relative sheet size indicated

P1 — pentagons, stars, boats, diamonds

P3 — the rhombs they produce

Every rhomb belongs to exactly one P1 tile. The tiles that emit no rhombs — stars, boats and diamonds — are the gaps, and they are invisible on the right precisely because they produce nothing. They are drawn on the left dashed, because those are the places a composite can be extended.

Scale is not asserted, it is derived: a pentagon's circumradius equals the rhomb edge exactly, which puts adjacent pentagons two apothems apart, edge to edge. Checked across every seed — no P1 tile overlaps another.
Joins. Each cut carries a tab shaped like the rhombus it joins to — the adjoining rhombus truncated at text height, a parallelogram because a rhombus's adjacent angles are supplementary. It is lettered with the sheet its other half is on: A▸3 tapes to A▸1 on sheet 3.

Every sheet carries a mini of the patch with its own rhombi filled in the sheet's colour and outlined along its cuts. The same colour keys this list, the mini, the Map, and the tiling canvas on the Workbench — so a sheet and its region read as one thing.

All sheets share one orientation, so the printed pages lie together the way they assemble. Print at 100% — no scaling, no fit-to-page — and measure a rhombus edge against the side length before cutting. Back side swaps hills for dales for the underside; reflection does not matter, since every patch here has a mirror axis.
